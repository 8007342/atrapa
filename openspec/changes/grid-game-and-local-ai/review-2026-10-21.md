# Design review: grid-game-and-local-ai (2026-10-21)

Reviewed: proposal.md, design.md, tasks.md, the five delta specs, plan/roadmap.md,
AGENTS.md, FORGE_DIAGNOSTICS.md. Code today: a render-only Flame canvas behind a
construction page and one widget test. Nothing in the change is implemented.

Facts this review relies on (from the operator, not from the forge envelope):
the training host is macuahuitl (RTX A5000 24 GB, 20-core i9, 62 GB RAM, NVIDIA
CDI for podman). The forge where Codex works has no GPU, 6 GiB RAM, a tmpfs
checkout, and forbids committed Python automation.

Overall verdict: the thinking is careful, but the package is a release plan
written before a prototype. 50 tasks across 7 groups, four new capabilities,
and the first thing a human can play arrives in group 3 after two review
gates that have no owner or deadline. Most of the complexity (actor
scheduling, deadline-tagged results, WASM-vs-Dart runtime bake-off, a live
training viewer, a fleet job API) solves problems that a 20K-parameter MLP
deciding four times per second does not have. Cut to a vertical slice, decide
the open rules now, and make every remaining task name the test that proves it.

Revisions are in priority order. Each gives the problem, the proposal, and
what it changes.

---

## R1. One rules core, in Dart, used by both the game and the trainer

**Problem.** design.md §7 says "reuse the same headless transition" but leaves
the trainer language open ("select a concrete supported trainer through a
narrow compatibility experiment", task 6.1). Every candidate except Dart
means a second simulator or a cross-language bridge, which is the drift risk
the design itself names (Risks: "Two simulation implementations drift").

**Options weighed.**

- *Rust core, native for the trainer, WASM for the browser.* Fastest rollouts
  and a path to burn/candle on the A5000. But the Flutter side would call the
  core from Dart through JS interop on web and through FFI on the five native
  runners: six build pipelines for the rules, state marshalled out of WASM
  every frame for Flame to draw, and wasm-bindgen glue Codex must build on a
  6 GiB forge with a tmpfs checkout. That is a lot of toolchain for a game
  whose rules fit in a few hundred lines.
- *Dart simulator, Python trainer.* Needs either a second simulator in Python
  or RPC into Dart per step. Forbidden to commit on the forge anyway.
- *Dart simulator, Dart trainer.* One language, one toolchain already working
  on the forge (Flutter 3.47 / Dart 3.13). The simulator is identical by being
  the same code. The policy is a tiny MLP; its training cost is dominated by
  rollouts, which are CPU work that Dart isolates spread across 20 cores on
  macuahuitl. The forge can run the whole pipeline at toy scale (seconds) as a
  CI smoke test. The A5000 is not needed for a 20K-parameter supervised
  model, and stays available later behind a data boundary (see R6).

**Proposal.** Pick Dart for both. Create a pure Dart package
`packages/atrapa_rules` (no `flutter`/`flame` imports) holding grid state,
transition, collision, command interpreter, observation encoder, and the MLP
evaluator. The Flutter app depends on it by path; the trainer is
`packages/atrapa_train`, a `dart run` command-line program depending on the
same package. Drift guard: one committed golden episode (seed, config,
command list, final state hash) replayed by a test in `atrapa_rules` and by a
widget test in the app. Write down the trigger for revisiting Rust: rollouts
slower than 1M steps/minute on macuahuitl after profiling, or a learner that
needs the GPU.

**Changes.** design.md §1, §7 (replace "select a trainer" with this decision);
tasks 2.1, 2.5, 6.1 (6.1 becomes "trainer smoke test in Dart"); spec
offline-policy-training "Shared authoritative simulation" (add: the rules
package has no engine dependency and the trainer imports it directly).

## R2. Replace the task list with a thin vertical slice first

**Problem.** Nothing is playable until group 3 of 7, and group 3 depends on
gates 1.2 and 1.3 that are "review with the user" with no proposed answers.
Groups 4–7 (41 tasks) are all downstream of an unmeasured assumption that
inference latency is a problem.

**Proposal.** Keep tasks.md, but prepend a "Slice A" section and mark
everything else "after Slice A". Slice A, in order, each step a mergeable PR:

1. `atrapa_rules`: square grid, N substeps per cell, heading, one queued turn
   consumed at the next boundary, constant speed, wall and other-snake
   collision, seeded RNG, 2–4 snakes, headless `step(dt)`. Golden replay test.
2. Dev route `/play` behind a flag: Flame surface drawing the grid, arrow keys,
   bottom touchpad in portrait, one human vs one scripted bot. Served by the
   existing Wrangler preview. Widget test at 320 px portrait.
3. Scripted bot: "avoid death, prefer open space" (look ahead K cells per
   candidate turn, choose the longest free run; random tie-break). This is
   the teacher and the permanent fallback.
4. `atrapa_train`: play the scripted bot against itself and record
   (observation, teacher action) pairs; train an MLP by supervised learning
   with plain SGD/Adam in Dart (`Float32List`, no boxing); export weights as a
   small JSON/binary asset with a manifest (schema version, shapes, hash).
5. The app loads the bundle, the Dart evaluator runs synchronously inside
   `update()` at each decision boundary, and the trained bot replaces the
   scripted bot for one opponent. Log µs per forward pass to the console.

Done when: a person can play against a trained bot in the browser preview on
a phone-sized window, and the console shows the forward-pass cost. Then
decide, from that number, whether any of group 4 is needed.

Deferred out of Slice A: acceleration/braking resources, powerups, tiers and
labels, actor scheduling, WASM, watch/replay viewer, live training feed,
tournaments, fleet job API, native runners.

**Changes.** tasks.md (new leading section; renumber nothing, just mark
groups 3.3–3.8, 4, 5.4–5.8, 6.4–6.9, 7 as post-slice); proposal.md "What
Changes" (state the slice explicitly); plan/roadmap.md first line.

## R3. Decide the open rules now instead of gating on a review

**Problem.** Tasks 1.2 and 1.3 and the spec requirement "Unresolved gameplay
gate" block implementation and training on a conversation that has no date,
owner, or proposed defaults. A gate without a proposed answer never closes.
The "Unresolved gameplay gate" requirement is also a process rule, not a
product behaviour; it cannot be tested.

**Proposal.** Replace the gates with v1 rules, stated as requirements, and
change them later through a new OpenSpec change if play says otherwise:

- Arrow key or pad press equal to the heading = boost request; opposite =
  brake request; perpendicular = turn request. Turn requests replace each
  other (latest wins) and one is consumed at the next boundary. Boost/brake
  apply while held (keyboard) or while the pad button is pressed; release
  ends them. (Slice A ignores boost/brake; the interpreter still parses them.)
- No growth and no self-collision in v1. Snakes have a fixed body length L
  from the level profile; body cells are obstacles for other snakes only.
- Collision resolution at a boundary: all intents computed from the
  pre-step state; a snake whose next head cell is a wall, a body cell, or
  another snake's next head cell loses; head swaps (A→B's cell while B→A's
  cell) both lose. Resolve all losses in the same step.
- Episode ends when at most one snake survives or after T ticks (profile);
  survivors at T are draws. Training reward is simply survival ticks plus a
  win bonus; the "endless circling" risk is handled by the opponent's
  presence, not by reward shaping, until measured otherwise.
- Powerups: v2.

**Changes.** Delete tasks 1.2/1.3 and the "Unresolved gameplay gate"
requirement; add the rules above to grid-gameplay spec as requirements with
one scenario each; design.md §3 shrinks to a pointer.

## R4. Inference runtime is the Dart evaluator; WASM only if a measurement fails

**Problem.** Spec local-policy-inference "Measured runtime selection" and
tasks 5.3–5.5 require building two backends (Dart and a WASM runtime), a
persistent worker, and a multi-device benchmark before choosing. For the
model in question this is a bake-off between a 40 KB asset and a multi-MB
runtime (onnxruntime-web ships 10–20 MB of wasm) to run roughly 20K
multiply-adds a few times per second.

**Proposal.** Dart evaluator is the runtime. Decisions happen at cell
boundaries, so at 4 substeps/cell and, say, 8 substeps/s there are two
decisions per snake per second; a 20K-MAC forward pass in dart2js is tens of
microseconds on a phone. Run it synchronously in `update()`. Record the p95
per decision from a real phone (one named floor device, see R7). Only if p95
exceeds a written budget (proposed: 2 ms per decision, all bots) does a
worker/WASM path get a task. Flutter web isolates are not available, so an
off-main-thread path means a JS web worker; that is a real cost and should
need evidence.

Model size target for phones: ≤ 25K parameters (≤ 100 KB FP32, ~50 KB as
float16), hard cap 100K. Suggested first architecture: observation = a 9×9
occupancy window around the head (3 channels: wall/body, other head, free)
plus heading one-hot, speed, and 4 ray distances → 256 inputs; two hidden
layers of 64; 4 outputs (coast/left/right/boost-or-brake). About 21K
parameters. Whole-bundle size counts (weights + manifest), but there is no
runtime to count with the Dart evaluator.

**Changes.** Spec local-policy-inference: rewrite "Measured runtime selection"
as "Runtime budget" (one scenario: p95 per decision on the floor device is
below the recorded budget, else the fallback bot plays and the overrun is
logged). Delete task 5.4; fold 5.5 into one measurement task; delete 5.7's
isolation-header work. design.md §6 last two paragraphs.

## R5. Drop the opponent-scheduling capability from the first release

**Problem.** The whole opponent-scheduling spec (bounded mailboxes, latest-
value broadcast, deadline-tagged results, stale-heading rejection, worker
supervision, overload telemetry) and design.md §4–5 are the design of an
asynchronous inference pipeline. With synchronous inference at boundaries
(R4) none of it exists. It also duplicates grid-gameplay in two places (see
R8).

**Proposal.** Move opponent-scheduling to a separate, not-yet-proposed change
("async-opponent-inference") whose entry condition is the measurement in R4
failing. Keep two sentences in design.md: decisions are taken synchronously
at boundaries from a snapshot taken before any snake's command is applied,
so completion order cannot leak decisions.

**Changes.** Remove specs/opponent-scheduling from this change; remove tasks
4.2–4.6; keep 4.1 (scripted bot) and move it into Slice A step 3.

## R6. Training runs on macuahuitl as a plain `dart run`; no fleet job API

**Problem.** design.md §7, spec "Verified offline compute allocation", task
6.5, and most of FORGE_DIAGNOSTICS' "training lane" section plan a remote job
submission system, pinned CUDA images, artifact transport, and a bounded live
episode channel. The forge cannot run any of it, and the operator has already
said what the training host is.

**Proposal.** Training is `dart run atrapa_train` on macuahuitl from a normal
checkout of branch `game`; output is a bundle committed under
`assets/policies/<name>/`. Keep datasets and checkpoints in an ignored
`.train/` directory. That is the entire job system for Slice A. Delete the
"Verified offline compute allocation" requirement; it is a statement about a
plan, not a behaviour, and the facts are now known. If a GPU learner is ever
wanted, the boundary is a dataset file (observations and actions as flat
binary) produced by the Dart rollout workers, consumed by whatever learner,
with the exported weights validated against the Dart evaluator on a fixed
corpus. The simulator never leaves Dart.

**Changes.** design.md §7; spec offline-policy-training (delete the
allocation requirement; "Reproducible policy bundles" stays); tasks 6.5, 6.7;
FORGE_DIAGNOSTICS' training-lane section becomes "not needed for Slice A".

## R7. Make unverifiable criteria verifiable

**Problem.** Many requirements promise measurement without a number or a
device: "declared devices", "documented tolerance", "declared tested
operating bounds", "representative real mobile devices". Tasks are mostly
"implement X; verify Y" with no artifact named, so a task can be ticked by
assertion.

**Proposal.**

- Name one floor device now (whatever Android phone the operator has at
  hand) and one desktop browser. Everything "on declared devices" means those
  two until a later change adds more.
- Tolerances: observation encoder parity is bitwise (same code, R1); exported
  weight round-trip is max abs error ≤ 1e-6 FP32, ≤ 1e-3 float16; action
  agreement on the fixed corpus ≥ 99.5% for float16.
- Every task names its proof: a test path (`packages/atrapa_rules/test/…`), a
  command (`dart run atrapa_train --smoke`), or a file it produces
  (`assets/policies/<name>/manifest.json`, `docs/measurements/<date>.md`).
- "Device-based runtime evidence" keeps only: cold load time, p95 decision
  time, and frame pacing during a 60 s match with 3 bots on the floor device.

**Changes.** specs local-policy-inference and offline-policy-training
(numbers in scenarios); every task line in tasks.md gains a "Proof:" suffix.

## R8. Remove overlaps between specs

**Problem.** The HARD/HARDER/HARDERER mapping is a requirement in both
grid-gameplay ("Level progression and opponent tiers") and opponent-scheduling
("Difficulty and speed calibration"). Simulation-time resource accounting is
in grid-gameplay ("Time and viewport fairness") and opponent-scheduling
("Time-based resource accounting"). Episode viewing appears in
offline-policy-training ("Selected episode viewing", "Observation does not
backpressure learning") and in grid-gameplay ("Responsive play and watch
presentation", watch half) and in design.md §8. Two specs disagreeing later
is the only possible outcome of saying the same thing twice.

**Proposal.** Tiers and labels live only in grid-gameplay. Time-based
accounting lives only in grid-gameplay. All watch/viewer/live-feed
requirements move to a separate future change ("training-viewer") together
with design.md §8 and tasks 3.7 (watch half), 3.8, 6.8, 6.9, 7.6. Slice A's
only "watch" is a replay of a golden episode in the dev route, which is a
test fixture, not a feature.

**Changes.** The four specs as named; tasks as named; proposal.md
capabilities list (drop the viewer from offline-policy-training's summary).

## R9. Smaller fixes

- grid-gameplay "Human and opponent controls": say explicitly that an arrow
  equal to the heading is boost and opposite is brake (R3); today the
  scenario only says "relative", which readers will take two ways.
- "Grid traversal": the 3–5 range belongs in the level profile schema (task
  1.4), not in the requirement; the requirement should say "a configured
  integer number of substeps". Keep 4 as the Slice A value.
- Spec atrapa delta is fine; add that the dev route is excluded from the
  production web build until a later change says otherwise.
- proposal.md "Impact": replace the A5000 sentence with "training runs on the
  operator's workstation; no remote jobs".
- AGENTS.md: add "rules live in packages/atrapa_rules; the app and the
  trainer import it; never reimplement a rule in lib/".
- FORGE_DIAGNOSTICS.md is useful but is a forge report, not a design
  document; the design should not cite it for decisions (design.md §8 does).

## Suggested order of edits

1. R3 (decide rules) and R1 (package layout) — these unblock everything.
2. R2 (Slice A section in tasks.md) and R5/R8 (move scheduling and viewer
   out) — the change becomes small enough to apply.
3. R4, R6, R7 — spec rewrites with numbers.
4. R9 — tidy.

After these edits, the change should be roughly: 3 specs (atrapa delta,
grid-gameplay, local-policy-inference), one offline-policy-training spec cut
to simulation sharing, bundles, parity and a small tournament, and about 15
tasks, five of which are Slice A.
